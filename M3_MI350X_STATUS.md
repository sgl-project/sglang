Gap to ATOM with real acceptance: **1.14x at c=24** (v8: 34,720 vs 39,680) and **1.18x at c=32** (v8: 35,935 reported vs 42,476; **1.08x on the in-window rate of 39,229**). **Like for like with ATOM's forced acceptance: 1.04x at c=24** (v11: 38,105 vs 39,680; v9-v12 repeats 37.2-38.1K, so the run-to-run spread is about 2%) **and 1.06x at c=32 as reported, 0.97x on the in-window rate** (v10: 39,976 reported vs 42,476; one stuck cancelled request added 300 s to AIPerf's denominator, while the 3,600 s window itself carried 43,641 tok/s/GPU, above ATOM's figure). Every c=32 run since v5 has hit this drain penalty (1-13 requests still streaming at the cutoff never return to the client within its 300 s timeout); c=24 runs drain cleanly. ATOM's figures use a forced acceptance length (`--spec-decode-acceptance-rate 0.5933`, performance-only: draft tokens commit without being checked against the target); v9 adds the same knob through SGLang's built-in `SGLANG_SIMULATE_ACC_LEN=2.78` (same floor/ceil schedule) so the two stacks are compared like for like. In isolation that knob plus 2 draft steps raises steady-state decode at 24 streams and 195K context from 1,990 to 2,704 tok/s (+35%), because the verify batch shrinks from 96 to 72 rows and one draft step disappears while the committed length stays 2.78. This morning it was 5.1x on the pinned client. At c=32 the prefill queue shows (TTFT p99 19 s vs 8.5 s at c=24).

\* v5's reported number is lower than v4's even though every per-request metric improved (ITL p50 10.3 -> 8.3 ms, E2E 12.4 -> 10.9 s, 3,173 vs 3,081 requests completed). AIPerf's `total_token_throughput` divides by the phase's active duration, which runs until the last cancelled in-flight request returns: in v5 two requests did not return within the 300 s drain timeout, so the denominator was 3,930 s instead of 3,630 s (-8%). Summing the per-request records over the 3,600 s window gives 32,850 for v5 vs 31,521 for v4 (+4.2%). The same happened to v7 at c=32 (8 in-flight requests stuck at the cutoff, 3,930 s denominator; 37,584 in-window vs 34,428 reported). ATOM's runs use the same client and are exposed to the same effect. Three runs (v5 c=32, v6 c=24 and c=32) aborted in warmup on a single client-side connection reset about 4-5 minutes in; raising the HTTP keep-alive timeout from uvicorn's 5 s default fixed it (v7 warmed up with zero resets). Same server scores 1.7x higher on the SA fork than on pinned aiperf 0.12.0 purely from client pacing, so only SA-fork numbers are comparable with ATOM.

## Where the remaining 1.17x / 1.23x is

The v7 c=24 run: avg E2E 10.2 s per request = TTFT 1.8 s + 992 output tokens x 8.5 ms effective ITL; lanes were 38% busy (about 15 s of client-side idle per request at the 300 s idle cap, same client flags as ATOM), and ISL keeps rising as the server gets faster (116K this morning -> 150K now) because lanes progress deeper into the traces. At this idle, ATOM's 39,680 corresponds to an E2E of roughly 7 s.

- Decode step at 24 requests / 195K context (isolated, HEAD): 26.5 ms. Measured floors: MoE fp4 8.6 ms runs at 6.4 TB/s, 80% of HBM peak (970 MB of expert weights per layer per rank at 96 rows); the custom all-reduce, 129 calls x 17 us = 2.6 ms, is the fastest variant available at these sizes (NCCL 48 us, 1-stage 26, quick-reduce 27, fused AR+norm neutral) and its count is fixed by TP; ~870 sub-8 us launches at the 4.3 us graph-replay floor = 4.0 ms; the rest is attention, GEMMs and norms near bandwidth. Only a different parallel layout (TP2 x DP2 or expert parallel, with cache-aware routing to keep the 97% prefix-hit rate) or aiter-side fusion of the MoE glue launches (1.0 ms) moves this further.
- Every AgentX turn is an extend of ~1.5K fresh tokens (median; mean 4.2K) over a 130-200K cached prefix, now replayed from prefill graphs (29-65 ms for 20-1.2K tokens); extends above ~3K tokens are GPU-bound.
- Prefill of long fresh prompts (21-37M fresh tokens per run): per 8192-token chunk at 192K after Tracks G/H, all-reduce 45-50 ms (INT4), MoE 44 ms, Gluon sparse attention 20 ms (L2-bound), dense attention 16 ms, bf16 dense GEMMs 26.5 ms (a PTPC-FP8 path exists opt-in but needs aiter tuning), rmsnorm 13 ms (at bandwidth). Every chunk stalls decode; mixed chunk is unavailable with speculative decoding.
- Quality gates hold on the recommended (real-acceptance) config: GSM8K-1000 0.854 (main branch this morning: 0.854 on 500 questions), GSM8K-500 0.86-0.89 across servers (run-to-run spread ~2.5 pt), greedy outputs identical between full prefill and cached restart apart from late synonym-level forks (different MoE batch shapes), needles coherent to 257K, 600 s stress with graph replay without faults. Every ATOM performance-only knob was tested: forced acceptance (adopted for v9 only, clearly labelled), INT4 quick-reduce (adopted, prefill-only, GSM8K 0.886), PTPC-FP8 dense (no speed gain here, GSM8K 0.828: rejected), chunk 16384 and mem 0.9 (adopted in v9 only: -5% prefill, +12% KV pool).

## Where the last 5% at c=24 is, and what was ruled out

At c=24 with forced acceptance the lanes are only 33% busy: each request spends ~8.3 s in flight (TTFT 2.1 s + 994 tokens x 6.2 ms) and ~16.5 s idle on the client's replayed inter-turn delays. Matching 39,680 needs ~7.3 s in flight. Ruled out today with measurements: scheduler knobs (Track K: `--schedule-policy lpm`, `--max-running-requests 32`, `--max-prefill-tokens 32768`, `--prefill-decode-interval 1`, `--schedule-conservativeness 0.3`, all within the +-2% run-to-run spread of 900 s windows; lpm + 32 running gave +1.1% at c=24 in a full run (v11) but hurts c=32, so it is not in the recommended config), prefill fairness for small turns behind long chunked prefills (Track L, env-gated, no TTFT gain in 900 s windows), chunk 16384 (neutral at c=24, +5% prefill speed on one long prompt), PTPC-FP8 dense (no gain), `--enable-aiter-allreduce-fusion` (slower), quick-reduce below 64 MB (never engages), MoE re-tuning (kernel already at 80% of HBM bandwidth), all-reduce variants (the 2-stage custom AR in use is the fastest at every decode size). What remains is structural: fewer all-reduce ranks per layer (TP2 x DP2 with cache-aware routing, or expert parallelism), aiter-side fusion of the MoE sorting/zero-fill/activation launches (1.0 ms of a 26.5 ms step), and an overnight aiter GEMM tuning run for the untuned bf16 shapes (~0.25 ms/step).
